Cutline

Cutline is an AI video tool that turns long podcasts, interviews and talks into short vertical clips, keeps only the moments worth posting, and publishes them to TikTok, Instagram and YouTube.

Role
Founder, Product Designer & Design Engineer
Timeline
2026 · ongoing
Project Type
Web App (SaaS)
Company
Cutline (my own product)

The Problem

A one-hour podcast holds maybe five moments worth posting. Finding them means scrubbing through the whole thing, cutting each one, reframing it for a phone, adding captions, writing a caption for every platform and posting it at the right time. Most creators either skip it or pay an editor.

AI clipping tools exist, but most of them flood you with 30 mediocre clips and leave you to sort through them. That just moves the work around.

So I set Cutline one promise: fewer, better clips. The AI should behave like a good editor, throwing away weak moments and explaining why, instead of padding the list.

How Cutline Works

You paste a link or upload videos, confirm you have the rights, pick a style, and press Generate. Cutline transcribes the video, finds the strongest moments, reframes them to vertical with captions, writes the post text, and lets you publish or schedule straight to your accounts. Then it learns from what performs.

The video at the top is a real run on a Creative Commons episode of Blocks Podcast with Neal Brennan and Russell Peters, with the processing time sped up.

1. Onboarding That Sets Up Every Project

Onboarding takes three quick steps and every answer changes how Cutline works. You pick the kinds of videos you clip, because the AI weighs moments differently for a podcast than for a sermon or a lesson. You set your default clip length and shape. Then you choose a caption style from live previews that show each word lighting up as it’s spoken.

Each screen explains why it’s asking, and everything can be changed later per project, so nobody gets stuck on a decision.

2. Start a Project in Seconds

The New project page is built so that every choice explains itself: clip length, aspect ratio with a plain note under each one, and editing styles like Minimal, Dynamic or Podcast that set captions, hook text, pause removal, zooms and framing in one click. If “Generate clips” is disabled, it says why.

Rights come first. Every project records whether you own the video, have a licence, or have the creator’s permission, because a public video isn’t automatically free to repurpose.

3. An AI Editor, Not a Clip Machine

Choosing moments happens in two passes. First the AI proposes about twice as many candidates as you asked for. Then an “executive producer” pass looks at frames from each one, drops weak, repetitive or confusing moments with a written reason, tightens the start and end points, and re-scores them. Only clips that pass a quality bar survive, and every clip starts and ends on a full sentence.

Nothing is hidden. If you asked for five clips and only three were strong, Cutline tells you, and shows you what it skipped and why.

4. Review and Polish Like an Editor

Each clip opens in an editor built for quick decisions. You can watch it, approve or reject it, and trim it by dragging handles or clicking a word in the transcript. Every clip shows why the AI picked it and a potential score broken down into hook, retention, storytelling and more, so you can judge it, not just trust it.

A caption designer with a live preview controls fonts, colours, position, words on screen, outline, box and pop-in animation. The post text is written for each platform and can be edited before posting.

5. Connect Your Accounts and Schedule Everything

Finding clips is only half the job; the other half is getting them out consistently. You connect your accounts once, through each platform’s own sign-in, and Cutline posts through the official APIs for YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and TikTok, with Facebook Reels on the way. Tokens are encrypted and refreshed automatically.

From any clip you can post now or pick a date and time per platform, each with its own AI-written caption. To plan a whole week, select several clips and schedule them in one go: choose the platform, the first post time and the spacing, such as one a day at 6 pm. Everything lands on a publishing calendar with an “Up next” list, and a post only counts as published once the platform confirms it. If an account isn’t connected yet, “Post it myself” prepares the video and caption so you can post by hand and mark it done.

6. Let It Learn What Works

Cutline pulls views, likes, comments and shares back from each platform at set intervals and learns which kinds of moments work for your audience. It tells you what to post next, builds a posting plan in one click, and only adjusts its picks when there’s enough real data, never by more than 15%.

Design Decisions

  • Fewer, better clips: A quality gate means you may get fewer clips than you asked for, on purpose, with the reasons shown.

  • Show the AI’s reasoning: Every clip explains why it was picked and every skipped moment explains why it wasn’t, so creators can trust the picks.

  • Rights by design: Every project records its rights, creator credits are added to captions automatically, and posting outside a licence is blocked.

  • Plain language everywhere: Settings, errors and processing steps are written for creators, not engineers, with no technical jargon.

What I Built

I designed Cutline and built it end to end with AI coding tools: a Next.js web app, a Python backend with its own job queue, Whisper transcription through Groq (a 42-minute video transcribes in 24 seconds), Gemini for choosing moments, FFmpeg and face detection for reframing and captions, and official publishing to YouTube and Instagram.

Where It Is Now

Cutline works end to end. An 11-minute video goes from upload to finished clips in about four minutes. I’ve published through it for real: Shorts to YouTube, including scheduled posts, and a public Reel to Instagram in about 35 seconds. It’s production-ready and preparing for launch, with Facebook Reels and TikTok next.

The biggest lesson: with AI products, what users trust most is honest judgement. Telling a creator “I only found three good moments, here’s why I skipped the rest” is worth more than thirty clips they have to sort through.
